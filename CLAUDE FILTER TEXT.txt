## Basic — the building blocks
- **Low-pass (LPF)** — passes below cutoff, attenuates above. The default "make it darker" filter.
- **High-pass (HPF)** — inverse. Cleans up mud, thins signals.
- **Band-pass (BPF)** — passes a window, cuts everything outside it.
- **Notch / band-reject** — inverse of band-pass. Surgical removal (hum, resonant peaks).
- **Shelving (low-shelf, high-shelf)** — boost/cut everything above or below a point, flat beyond it. This is what EQ "bass/treble" knobs actually are.
- **Peak/bell (parametric)** — boost/cut centered on a frequency with adjustable width. The workhorse of every EQ plugin you've ever touched.
- **All-pass** — passes everything in magnitude, only shifts phase. Sounds like nothing alone, but it's the secret ingredient in phasers and Hilbert-transform tricks.

## Slope / order variants
Same shapes above, different steepness: 1-pole (6dB/oct), 2-pole (12dB/oct), 4-pole (24dB/oct — the classic Moog ladder slope), up through steep "brickwall" digital filters. Steeper = more phase distortion near the cutoff, generally.

## Classic analog-modeled topologies — where it gets interesting
This is the layer that actually matters for you as a plugin builder, since these are implementation choices, not just EQ curves:
- **Moog transistor ladder** — 24dB/oct LP, self-oscillates at high resonance, nonlinear saturation baked into the feedback path. THE "fat synth filter" sound.
- **Diode ladder (TB-303)** — more aggressive, screamier resonance than the Moog ladder. Acid house exists because of this filter.
- **Sallen-Key** — cleaner resonance behavior (ARP/Oberheim-style), less "growl" than ladder designs.
- **State Variable Filter (SVF)** — outputs LP/BP/HP/notch simultaneously from one structure. Digitally stable, mod-friendly — this is what most modern soft synths actually implement under the hood.
- **Steiner-Parker** — another classic multimode, fatter and slightly different resonant character than SVF.
- **Biquad (RBJ Cookbook / Direct Form)** — the actual math most software filters reduce to. Not a "character" filter itself, but the implementation layer for everything above.

## Character / creative filters
- **Self-oscillating resonance** — push resonance far enough and the filter becomes an oscillator. Free sine wave from a filter you already have.
- **Formant filter** — stacks resonant bandpasses to mimic vowel shapes (ah/ee/oh). Vocal-adjacent timbre without a vocal.
- **Vocoder / filter bank** — array of narrow bandpass filters extracts one signal's amplitude envelope, imposes it on another. Robot voice, but really it's just filtering at scale.
- **Comb filter** — feedforward version gives metallic, flangey coloration; feedback version is literally Karplus-Strong plucked-string synthesis.
- **Phaser** — cascaded all-pass filters with LFO-swept center frequencies. Sweeping notches, no amplitude buildup.
- **Flanger** — short modulated delay = swept comb filter. "Jet plane" sound, harmonically locked peaks/nulls (unlike phaser).
- **Auto-wah / envelope filter** — bandpass cutoff driven by an envelope follower on the input. Funk in a box.
- **Wah pedal** — manually swept resonant bandpass. Same idea, foot-controlled.

## Extreme / experimental
- **Chaotic/nonlinear feedback filters** — self-oscillating filters driven past stability into FM-like chaos. Used in West Coast/Buchla-style synthesis.
- **Spectral (FFT) filtering** — shape magnitude/phase directly in the frequency domain. Enables things time-domain filters physically can't do — spectral freeze, spectral gating, cross-bin morphing.
- **Convolution filtering** — apply any impulse response as a "filter." The most general filter category that exists — a room, an amp, a broken speaker, anything you can capture as an IR.
- **Cross-synthesis / spectral morphing** — impose one sound's spectral envelope on another. Vocoder taken to its logical extreme.
- **Frequency shifter (Hilbert transform / SSB)** — technically all-pass-derived, but instead of multiplying frequencies (pitch shift) it adds a fixed Hz offset to all of them, destroying harmonic relationships. Alien, metallic, "barberpole" effect.
- **Physical-modeling waveguide filters** — feedback delay networks tuned to model resonant physical objects (strings, tubes, drumheads).
- **Morphing multimode filters** — continuous blend across LP→BP→HP→notch via a single control, common in modern digital synths that don't want to commit to one topology.
